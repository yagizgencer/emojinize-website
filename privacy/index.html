Privacy policy

Last updated 3 November 2026 · Version 1.1

Your text stays yours

Only the text you emojinize is sent. It is used to pick emoji and is never stored.

No account, no cookies, no ID. We never know who you are or which pages you visit. Your IP address is used only to keep the service fair, and is never stored.

What we measure

To keep Emojinize fast and working, each use sends a short anonymous summary, built only from fixed categories:

  • which feature you used, and whether it worked
  • the language, how many words got an emoji, and how quickly, as rough ranges
  • whether it is your first use that day, week or month, so we can count users without an ID
  • the extension version, browser and display mode
  • the most shown emoji in each language
  • how often the feedback form is offered, opened or put off
  • how often an earlier result could be reused instead of asking our server again, as counts

Our server also counts how much text it handles and how long it takes.

None of this includes your text or anything that identifies you. We never sell or share data, and never use it to train models.

On your device

Your settings and a few counters, like when you last used Emojinize, stay in your browser. Uninstalling removes them.

Recent results are kept in your browser's memory, so the same words are not sent twice. Short text you meet often, like a site's menus and buttons, is also kept on disk so this lasts between sessions. Both are stored as fingerprints and emoji, never as your text. The memory copy is gone when you close the browser. The disk copy is replaced as it fills up and removed when you uninstall. Nothing from private windows is kept on disk.

Feedback you send

Messages sent through our feedback form are kept by Emojinize so we can read them, apart from the usage statistics. Each one carries its topic, the website you named if any, the extension version and where the form was opened from. Nothing from the pages you read is attached.

To keep out spam, the form checks that a person is sending it with Cloudflare Turnstile, which looks at your browser for that moment. We get only its yes or no.
